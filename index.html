Master's Thesis · Research in progress · 2026

Digital Pathology Research

Deep Learning Detection and Quantification of SARIFA in Colorectal Cancer Whole-Slide Images

Computer vision · Attention-based learning · Spatial analysis · Reproducible research

Can a model find a small, clinically relevant tissue pattern inside a gigapixel image? SARIFA describes direct contact between tumor glands or cell clusters and fat cells at the tumor's invasive edge, without intervening reactive tissue. It is associated with poorer cancer outcomes. My project predicts whether this pattern is present and investigates where it occurs, rather than predicting survival or producing a validated tissue segmentation.

I built the Python/PyTorch pipeline from slide preprocessing and pathologist annotations through model training, patient-level evaluation, and spatial review. The central comparison is between a simple rule that averages the highest-scoring 1% of image tiles and a model that learns which tiles to attend to. Both use the same annotation-trained ResNet-50 encoder, allowing the aggregation method to be studied separately from the image representation.

TCGA colorectal cancer cohort

01 · Representation

Learn from partial annotations

ResNet-50 classifies 512 × 512-pixel tissue tiles. Annotated positive regions and negative slides provide supervision. Unannotated tissue on positive slides is not assumed negative: it is excluded from the tile loss but retained in the later patient-level models.

02 · Aggregation

Compare fixed and learned pooling

Gated attention-based multiple-instance learning (ABMIL, Ilse et al.) learns to weight tile embeddings using patient labels. An annotation-ranking variant additionally encourages annotated positive tiles to receive higher relevance scores than uncertain tissue from the same slide, without labelling that tissue negative.

03 · Interpretation

Inspect the tissue behind a prediction

Tile-score heatmaps and paired attention maps compare model emphasis with pathologist regions. Attention indicates relative weighting, not the probability of SARIFA in a tile. Current finishing work measures annotated area and maximum within-slide SARIFA extent, separately from thresholded model-derived candidate regions.

Initial patient-level results

Internal test: 33 patients, including 12 SARIFA-positive cases.
Method AUROC (95% CI) AUPRC
Fixed top-1%, original tile cap 0.849 (0.683–0.956) 0.821
Fixed top-1%, uncapped 0.853 (0.687–0.956) 0.818
Plain ABMIL, uncapped 0.806 (0.615–0.940) 0.791
Annotation-ranking ABMIL, uncapped 0.813 (0.631–0.933) 0.776

AUROC measures ranking across classes; AUPRC summarizes positive-case retrieval. Higher is better, but neither is classification accuracy. Intervals use 1,000 patient-bootstrap resamples; ABMIL results use five-seed probability ensembles. The original cap limited negative slides to 5,000 tiles; the uncapped analysis includes all eligible tissue tiles.

The simpler baseline remained competitive. Both ABMIL ensembles reached validation AUROC 0.953, but their advantage did not carry over to test. Annotation ranking concentrated more attention on annotated tissue without a clear gain in patient-level discrimination. The cohort is small, and paired uncertainty intervals do not establish superiority. These are secondary model comparisons on the same internal test cohort first evaluated with the fixed baseline, not independent clinical validation.

Engineering and study design

Reproducible experiments. Stage contracts, fixed patient splits (141 training / 28 validation / 33 test), hashed inputs and checkpoints, and structured results.
Controlled comparisons. Removing the negative-slide tile cap expanded the field from 4.2 to 10.5 million tiles and removed a label-linked cap on bag size before the main ABMIL comparison.
Pathology-informed evaluation. Pathologist review, patient-level error analysis, and checks of annotation burden and spatial localization alongside headline metrics.
Practical implementation. PyTorch, OpenSlide, NumPy, Shapely and Parquet; reusable tile embeddings, bounded-memory processing, and recorded verification checks.

Next: external Finnish biobank evaluation

Preparation is in progress for an independent Finnish colorectal cancer biobank cohort of approximately 1,354 patients. The locked tile-classifier and the aggregation pipeline will be tested on local patients under data-protection restrictions, without using external outcomes to retune the model.

Thesis and manuscript in preparation. Research software, not a clinical diagnostic tool.

About Me

Beyond the Work

I care about correctness more than cleverness. I'd rather build a simple, well-validated pipeline I can defend in a methods section than an elaborate one I can't fully explain. That instinct comes from scientific training - where you're accountable for your methodology, not just your results - and it travels with me into every engineering problem I touch.

What I find most interesting is working at the boundary between disciplines: where biological questions meet computational methods, where a model's output has to mean something to a clinician, where the right answer requires understanding both sides. That's the space I've spent years building toward, and where I do my best work.

The same instinct carries outside work. I'd rather ski a long trail through forest than a lifeless slope, ride proper bush on a mountain bike, and find the good coffee in a city before the obvious one. I read widely - history, philosophy, science - because the most useful ideas rarely stay in their original field.
